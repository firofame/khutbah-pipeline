Do not silently correct or authenticate a source quotation. If a phrase is unclear or appears garbled, use the narrowest meaning supported by context; do not invent missing content.

Keep deliberate repetition and rhetorical questions when they contribute to the speech; remove accidental duplication only when it is clearly a transcription error.
- Keep key Islamic terms in familiar Malayalam forms where appropriate, and explain their sense naturally when needed: അല്ലാഹു, തഖ്‌വ, തവക്കുൽ, റിസ്ഖ്, ഹലാൽ, ഹറാം, ദുനിയാവ്, ആഖിറം. Do not force Arabic loanwords where ordinary Malayalam is clearer.
- Translate Qur'anic verses and hadiths faithfully by their meaning in Malayalam. Introduce them naturally when the source does so, for example: അല്ലാഹു തആലാ വിശുദ്ധ ഖുർആനിൽ പറയുന്നു. Preserve a hadith attribution such as ബുഖാരി നിവേദനം ചെയ്ത ഹദീസിൽ only when the transcript explicitly provides it.
- Render honorifics and blessings in readable Malayalam. For example, write നബി സ്വല്ലല്ലാഹു അലൈഹി വസല്ലം and റദിയല്ലാഹു അൻഹു in Malayalam script when the source includes those honorifics. Preserve their intent without adding an honorific to people whom the source does not honor.
- Preserve important names and places accurately. Transliterate names into conventional Malayalam spelling. Do not infer a khatib's name from a previous sermon or from a default value.
- Make the result suitable for continuous audio narration. Use short paragraphs and sensible sentence breaks. Do not include translator notes, summaries, section headings, stage cues, or descriptions of recitation.
